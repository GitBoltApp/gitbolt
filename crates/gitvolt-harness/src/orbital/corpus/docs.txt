# Orbital

![The Orbital title screen](assets/title/title.png)

A small space-trading game about orbits, cargo and bad decisions. You fly a scout between
the planets of one system, mine rock for ore, and sell it where it fetches the most.

## The play loop

1. Undock from the station and fly out into the system.
2. Fly through an asteroid belt. The laser mines any rock in range into the hold.
3. Dock with a station. Sell the ore at its market and buy what the next run needs.
4. Check the orbit map before you burn, so you know where the planets are.

## Features

- Flight under the pull of every planet, with a fixed-step simulation that gives the same
  result on every machine.
- A mining laser that cuts any rock within 40 km into a twelve-slot hold. Hitting a rock wears
  both hulls down, and a rock worn through explodes.
- Three stations, each trading twelve goods at prices that move as you buy and sell.
- An orbit map of the whole system, which the ship keeps flying under.
- A title screen, and saves in `.orbsave` files.
- The system, the ships and the goods come from the tables in `assets/data/`.

## Requirements

- Rust 1.85. `rust-toolchain.toml` pins it, so `rustup` picks it up on its own.
- A GPU that wgpu can drive: Vulkan, Metal or DirectX 12.

## Building

    cargo run -p orbital-game

Run the tests with `cargo test --workspace` from the top of the workspace.

# Orbital

![The Orbital title screen](assets/title/title.png)

A small space-trading game about orbits, cargo and bad decisions. You fly a scout between
the planets of one system, mine rock for ore, take delivery contracts, and sell where it
fetches the most.

## The play loop

1. Undock from the station and fly out into the system.
2. Fly through an asteroid belt. The laser mines any rock in range into the hold.
3. Dock with a station. Sell the ore at its market, take a contract if one pays, and buy what
   the next run needs.
4. Refuel and repair before you leave. Repairs cost by the percentage of hull lost.
5. Check the orbit map before you burn, so you know where the planets are.

## Features

- Flight under the pull of every planet, with a fixed-step simulation that gives the same
  result on every machine.
- A mining laser that cuts any rock within 40 km into the hold. Hitting a rock wears both hulls
  down, and a rock worn through explodes.
- Three stations, each keeping its own market of thirteen goods. Prices move as you trade and
  drift back towards their base once a game day.
- Delivery contracts with deadlines, paid by distance and urgency, and kept in the save.
- Refuelling and repairs at every station.
- An orbit map of the whole system, with labels and a legend, which the ship keeps flying under.
- Gamepad support: the left stick steers and gives analogue thrust.
- Settings for the music volume, the light on the planets, the thrust key, the text size, high
  contrast and a colour-blind palette.
- An autosave every five minutes of flying, which Continue on the title screen picks up.
- Ship classes from mod folders, listed in the shipyard on the docked screen.

## Prices

Every trade moves the price of that good at that station, and the station remembers it after
you leave. Once a game day every market takes a small random shake and drifts back towards its
usual stock. A contract pays by distance and by how close its deadline is.

## Requirements

- Rust 1.85. `rust-toolchain.toml` pins it, so `rustup` picks it up on its own.
- A GPU that wgpu can drive: Vulkan, Metal or DirectX 12.
- On Linux, the udev headers (`libudev-dev` on Debian and Ubuntu), which the gamepad library
  builds against.

## Building

    cargo run -p orbital-game

Run the tests with `cargo test --workspace` from the top of the workspace.

## Controls

The keys are listed in [docs/controls.md](docs/controls.md). The main ones:

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| W, A, D | Thrust, turn left, turn right |
| U | Undock |
| B, S | Buy and sell a tonne of the selected good |
| C | Accept the first contract a station offers |
| R | Refuel, and repair the hull, at a station |
| T | Repair the hull at a station |
| M | Open the orbit map |
| P | Pause the flight |
| F3, F5 | Frame-time overlay, reload the sprite shader |

## Saves

Saves are `.orbsave` files, written to a temporary file and renamed into place. The format
is in [docs/saves.md](docs/saves.md). There is a sample save in `saves/` for bug reports.

## Modding

Ship classes can be added with a mod folder. See [docs/modding.md](docs/modding.md).

## Contributing

See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md). Bugs and ideas go in the issue tracker on
https://gitlab.example.com/orbital/orbital.

# Contributing

Thanks for looking at Orbital. The team is small, so the rules are short.

## Branches

Work on a branch named after the feature, such as `feature/gravity-coarse`. Keep `main`
working: every commit on it builds and passes the tests.

## Merge requests

Open a merge request against `main` on https://gitlab.example.com/orbital/orbital. Ask one
person who has not touched the change to review it. The branch lands as one commit.

## Tests and lints

    cargo test --workspace
    cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D clippy::all

CI runs both on every push, with `cargo fmt --check` as well. A change that breaks any of
them does not merge. Clippy's default lints are errors; the pedantic ones each crate turns on
are warnings, to clear in the code you touch.

## Commit messages

Write a plain sentence in the imperative, such as "Fix the docking chime playing twice".
Start it with a capital, keep it under 72 characters, and leave off the full stop. Do not
use prefixes like `feat:`.

# Architecture

Orbital is a Cargo workspace. For now it holds one crate, `orbital-core`: the simulation,
with no rendering and no window. The render layer and the game come in the next steps.

## Units

Distances are in kilometres, times in seconds and masses in tonnes. Angles are in radians.
The header of `crates/orbital-core/src/lib.rs` has the same note.

# Architecture

Orbital is a Cargo workspace with three crates. Each one depends only on the ones above it
in the diagram.

```mermaid
flowchart TD
  game["orbital-game<br/>app, flight, docked, menu, map_view, trade_screen, settings, save_slots, audio"]
  render["orbital-render<br/>renderer, painter, camera, sprite_batch, atlas, text, starfield"]
  core["orbital-core<br/>body, world, level, ship, station, cargo, economy, save"]
  systems["systems<br/>gravity, thrust, collisions, mining, docking, economy"]
  game --> render
  game --> core
  render --> core
  core --> systems
```

## Crates

- `orbital-core` is the simulation. A system's objects live in a `World`: one table per
  component, with an `Entity` index into each. The systems read and write those tables once
  per tick, in a fixed order. The bodies of a new game come from the sector tables in
  `assets/data/levels/`, read by `level.rs`.
- `orbital-render` draws a `World` through `wgpu`. It knows nothing about the rules of the game.
- `orbital-game` owns the window, the input, the screens and the save slots, and calls the
  other two.

Distances are in kilometres, times in seconds and masses in tonnes. Angles are in radians.

# Architecture

Orbital is a Cargo workspace with three crates. Each one depends only on the ones above it
in the diagram.

```mermaid
flowchart TD
  game["orbital-game<br/>app, flight, docked, menu, map_view, trade_screen, settings, save_slots, audio"]
  render["orbital-render<br/>renderer, painter, camera, sprite_batch, atlas, text, starfield"]
  core["orbital-core<br/>body, world, level, ship, station, cargo, economy, save"]
  systems["systems<br/>gravity, thrust, collisions, mining, docking, economy"]
  buses["audio buses<br/>effects, interface, music"]
  game --> render
  game --> core
  game --> buses
  render --> core
  core --> systems
```

## Crates

- `orbital-core` is the simulation. A system's objects live in a `World`: one table per
  component, with an `Entity` index into each. The systems read and write those tables once
  per tick, in a fixed order. The bodies of a new game come from the sector tables in
  `assets/data/levels/`, read by `level.rs`.
- `orbital-render` draws a `World` through `wgpu`. It knows nothing about the rules of the game.
- `orbital-game` owns the window, the input, the screens and the save slots, and calls the
  other two.

## Sound

The game asks for a sound by name, and each sound plays on a bus. The flight's sounds are on
the effects bus, the menus' clicks are on the interface bus, and the music has its own gain.
A bus can be balanced on its own, so the mix can change without touching the sounds.

The audio device is a stub for now: `audio::output` writes each sound and its bus to the log at
trace level, so a sound's trigger can be checked before the output stream lands.

## Units

Distances are in kilometres, times in seconds and masses in tonnes. Angles are in radians.

# Save files

A save is one `.orbsave` file. The game writes it to a temporary file first and then renames
the file over the old save. Every number is little-endian.

| Field | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| magic | 4 bytes | `ORBS` |
| version | `u32` | `1` in this format |
| tick | `u64` | the game clock, in physics ticks |
| credits | `f32` | the player's money |
| ship x, ship y | `f32` each | position |
| ship vx, ship vy | `f32` each | velocity |
| ship mass | `f32` | tonnes |
| cargo | 12 slots of `u8` then `f32` | the good's id (255 when the slot is empty), then its tonnes |
| checksum | `u32` | CRC-32 (IEEE, as zlib uses) of every byte before it |

A file shorter than 16 bytes is refused as truncated before it is read, and a file whose
checksum doesn't match is refused as damaged.

The sample save in `saves/sample.orbsave` is in this format.

# Save files

A save is one `.orbsave` file. The game writes it to a temporary file first and then renames
the file over the old save. Every number is little-endian.

| Field | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| magic | 4 bytes | `ORBS` |
| version | `u32` | `3` in this format |
| created | `u64` | when the save was made, in seconds since the Unix epoch |
| tick | `u64` | the game clock, in physics ticks |
| credits | `f32` | the player's money |
| ship x, ship y | `f32` each | position |
| ship vx, ship vy | `f32` each | velocity |
| ship mass | `f32` | tonnes |
| ship fuel | `f32` | fuel left in the tank |
| ship hull | `f32` | hull left |
| cargo | 12 slots of `u8` then `f32` | the good's id (255 when the slot is empty), then its tonnes |
| contracts | `u32` count, then the contracts | see below |
| checksum | `u32` | CRC-32 (IEEE, as zlib uses) of every byte before it |

Each contract is five fields: the good's id `u8`, its tonnes `f32`, the destination's station
index `u32`, its reward `f32`, and the ticks left `u32`.

Version 1 files load with no creation time, a full tank and a whole hull. Version 2 files
load with no contracts. The game writes version 3 only.

A file shorter than 16 bytes is refused as truncated before it is read, and a file whose
checksum doesn't match is refused as damaged. An empty slot, with no file or an empty one, is
not an error: Continue starts a new game from it.

The sample save in `saves/sample.orbsave` is in this format.

# Modding

A mod can add ship classes. Make a folder under `mods/`, in the folder the game runs from, next
to `saves/`, and put a `ships.csv` in it:

    mods/
      my-mod/
        ships.csv

The file has the same columns as `assets/data/ships.csv`, under the same header row:

    class,mass_t,cargo_t,thrust_kn,fuel_t,price
    freighter,5.0,360,750,90,9800

The masses are in tonnes and the thrust in kilonewtons. For a feel of the numbers, the scout is
2 tonnes with a 600 kN drive and a 60 tonne tank.

The game reads the mod folders once, at startup, in the order of their names. A line it cannot
read is skipped, and the game writes a line to the log naming the file and the line. Class names
must be unique across the base game and every mod; a class whose name is taken is skipped the same
way.

The classes show in the shipyard list on the docked screen, with their prices, so you can check
that a mod loaded. They can't be bought or flown yet: every game starts in the scout.

# Trade guide

Ore is the first good most players sell, so this guide follows a few tonnes of it. Ore has a base
price of 12 credits a tonne, from `assets/data/economy/commodities.csv`.

## Buy and sell

Each station keeps a stock of each good, and keeps it between your visits. A station likes to
hold 20 tonnes of each. The price moves with the stock:

- The price to buy is the base price, plus 2 percent of the base for each tonne the station is
  short of 20, minus 2 percent for each tonne it has over. A buy price never goes below one credit.
- The price to sell is 90 percent of the buy price, so a sale always pays less than a purchase
  would cost at the same moment.

Buying takes stock away, so the price goes up. Selling adds stock, so the price goes down.

## Drift

Once a game day, a little under fifteen minutes of flying, every station's stock moves on its
own. First it takes a small random shake: up to the good's volatility from the commodity table,
as a share of 20 tonnes, either way. Ore's volatility is 0.04, so its shake is under a tonne.
Then 5 percent of the gap between the stock and 20 closes. A station that has been bought from
settles back over a week or two, and the price settles with it.

## A worked example

Say a station holds 10 tonnes of ore. Its buy price is 12 times (1 + 0.02 times 10), which is 14.40
credits a tonne, and its sell price is 90 percent of that, 12.96.

You buy 5 tonnes. The cost is 5 times 14.40, so 72 credits, and the station is left with 5 tonnes.
Its buy price is now 15.60, because the stock is further below 20. You do not pay the new price for
the tonnes you have just bought.

Seven game days later the stock has drifted back. Each day closed 5 percent of what was left of the
gap, so after a week about 30 percent of the gap of 15 tonnes has closed. Leaving the shakes out,
the station holds about 9.5 tonnes, and its buy price is about 14.50 again.

So if you wanted ore for a run, buying it at the station straight after a sale costs more than
buying it a week later. Look at the trade screen before you buy, and compare the buy price with
the base.

## Contracts

The contracts in the design document pay a fixed reward on delivery, so drift will not change what
a contract pays. It will change what the ore in your hold would sell for on the open market while
you fly, so a contract with a long deadline gives you time to wait for a better market.

# Adding a translation

Every string the interface shows lives in one table, `STRINGS` in
`crates/orbital-game/src/strings.rs`. The menus, the settings screen and the HUD look their text
up by key with `tr`, so a translation is that table with the second column changed.

## Steps

1. Copy the table and translate the second column. Keep the keys as they are.
2. Keep each string in capitals. The HUD font has capitals, digits, the space and a few marks
   (`. : % / -`), and a character it doesn't have is drawn as a gap.
3. Keep strings short. A menu or settings row has room for about sixteen characters.
4. Run the game and go through the title screen, the settings screen and a flight.

A key the table has no string for shows the key itself, such as `menu.quit`. That is on purpose:
a missing string is easy to spot on screen, and easy to search for.

## Not in the table yet

The docked screen's key hints, the trade panel's lines and the contract rows still have their
text in the code. They move into the table next, with the station names.

There is no way to pick a language in the game yet. For now a translation replaces the table.
